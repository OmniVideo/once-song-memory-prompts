# Choosing details for lyrics

Guidance aligned with what [Once Song](https://oncesong.com/) says makes a song feel personal — without inventing site features.

## Prefer scenes over labels

| Weaker | Stronger |
| --- | --- |
| “You are kind” | “You waited at the station in the rain with an extra umbrella” |
| “Funny friend” | “You kept our dog’s nickname in every group chat” |
| “Great parent” | “You still hang the crooked star we made when I was five” |

The homepage and FAQ call out names, shared memories, meaningful places, favorite qualities, inside jokes, promises, and a message from the heart.

## Name in the chorus

The product story emphasizes the recipient’s name woven into the song / chorus. Write the **exact spelling and preferred nickname**. Add a pronunciation note for uncommon names when the form allows free text.

## How many memories?

A few short, vivid memories usually travel into lyrics better than one long autobiography. Aim for 2–5 concrete moments. Skip addresses, ID numbers, and secrets that would hurt if overheard.

## Separate facts from wishes

- **Facts / memories:** what happened.
- **Message:** thank you, I love you, I’m proud of you.
- **Feeling choices:** mood, genre, voice — selected on the live form, not guessed here.

## Creative variation (from Terms)

A custom song reflects your questionnaire details and creative direction, but exact wording, pacing, instrumentation, mood, or resemblance to a reference cannot be guaranteed. The private preview is your chance to evaluate before unlock.

## Privacy while drafting

Anyone with the song identifier / private link can open the song page. Do not put passwords, payment data, or highly sensitive third-party stories into the form. See Privacy Policy and `preview-and-gift-checklist.md`.
